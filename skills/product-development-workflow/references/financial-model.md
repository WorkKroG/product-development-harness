# Viability and Financial Decisions

Build economic evidence around the next decision, not a fundraising narrative or a desired output. Facts, observed data, external benchmarks, estimates, hypotheses, and unknowns must remain distinguishable. Record source and date for external facts and never fabricate numbers.

## Gate 3.5: light viability

Gate 3.5 is a short, approximate checkpoint after Positioning and before Journey. It asks whether the next bounded use of time or money is justified and what uncertainty to test first. A concise section in an existing discovery artifact is enough.

Cover:

- **accessible market:** an order-of-magnitude segment and the part realistically reachable for the next experiment, with a plausible first channel;
- **competitors and substitutes:** meaningful direct alternatives and the current workaround, available price signals, and why a user may switch;
- **payer or value:** who pays and for what in a commercial product, or who receives value and what cost is acceptable in an internal or non-commercial product;
- **broad income and cost ranges:** approximate price or income where applicable, development, acquisition, operation, and one-time or recurring cost ranges relevant to the next step;
- **strongest unknown:** the assumption most capable of changing the decision;
- **one bounded experiment:** the smallest evidence-producing next step, its resource boundary, result threshold, and revisit condition.

Broad ranges with explicit assumptions are preferable to false precision. A workbook is not required; exact CAC/LTV is not required. A universal Month-24 target is not required. Never fabricate numbers to fill a gap. When no defensible estimate exists, mark it unknown and design the bounded experiment.

Finish with exactly one decision: proceed to Journey, run the experiment, change the segment/value/model, or stop. Approval of that decision authorizes only the named next step and its stated resources.

## Gate 8: investment-proportionate finance

Reuse unchanged Gate 3.5 evidence and deepen the economics only enough for the named investment. Do not repeat competitor research, market sizing, channel evidence, or other inputs that remain current. Refresh affected inputs when scope, price, monetization, channel, cost, risk control, architecture, time, or observed behavior changes them.

Name the decision and choose proportional depth:

- **bounded prototype:** cost ceiling, expected learning value, major variable costs, and stop/revisit trigger;
- **paid pilot:** willingness-to-pay evidence, payment and refund behavior, variable cost, contribution range, cash boundary, and support burden;
- **permanent commitment:** retention or repeat-value evidence, fixed and step costs, cash need, break-even range, risks, and exit options;
- **acquisition scaling:** observed acquisition stage and cost, contribution, retention, payback, channel capacity, cash headroom, and guardrails;
- **internal or non-commercial investment:** expected value or capacity improvement, delivery and operating costs, alternatives, budget boundary, and review trigger.

Use scenarios and sensitivity only where they can change the named decision. A detailed driver-based workbook may be warranted for high spend, long commitments, uncertain cash timing, multiple revenue streams, or acquisition scaling; it is not a universal gate artifact.

For every material input record its unit, time window, evidence class, source or collection plan, range, and owner when applicable. Keep revenue and associated costs in the same period; include taxes, payment costs, refunds, consumable product costs, support, infrastructure, team, security, migration, and rollback when they materially affect the decision.

Finish with proceed, cheaper experiment, changed price/scope/channel/cost, deferred commitment, or stop. Record the investment boundary, accepted risk, and next recalculation date or evidence trigger.
